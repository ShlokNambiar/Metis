The challenge

Can we trust what the backtest tells us?

Pepl Life brought us something valuable and fragile in equal measure: a proprietary end-of-session strategy with promising discretionary results, an early codebase of uneven quality, and one hard question.

The retail-quant world is littered with systems that look brilliant in backtests and lose money live. The failure modes are always the same: look-ahead bias, fabricated or forward-filled data, optimistic fills, risk numbers that understate exposure, and validation gates that bless statistical noise. A previous review of the client's codebase claimed 172 issues fixed; our independent verification found those claims substantially overstated. That set the tone for the whole engagement.

Build a platform whose numbers can be trusted before building a platform that does more things.

Our approach

Trust before features.

01

Strategy-agnostic engine

The client's strategy is IP — the platform never depends on it or special-cases it. The core is a clean event-driven engine where any strategy plugs in as an isolated “Scout.” It became a commercial asset: bring-your-own-strategy onboarding for third parties.

02

Faithful data or no data

No synthetic candles, no forward-fills across sessions, no interpolated gaps sold as market truth. Every backtest runs on real exchange data through validated pipelines that reject, rather than repair, anything suspicious.

03

Fail closed, always

When data is missing or a fit won't converge, the system refuses or degrades conservatively — never an optimistic number. A risk figure that silently reads 0.0 is the most dangerous class of bug in finance, and we hunted it relentlessly.

04

Adversarial verification

Every delivery was followed by an independent audit wave: unbiased reviewers re-deriving the math from first principles and trying to break the code. Every fix had to fail on the old code before it counted.

What we built

Seven phases, from trusted core to production.

01

A backtest core worth trusting

A deterministic event lifecycle with provable no-look-ahead semantics, realistic gap-through-stop fills, explicit transaction-cost modelling, and a single risk authority every order passes through. The strategy was ported to the Scout interface byte-for-byte faithful to spec.

02

Real market data, end to end

Ingestion for NSE BhavCopy, intraday options, and broker historical APIs with point-in-time correctness, corporate-action handling, calendar validation, and quality gates that fail loudly. Disagreeing sources reconcile conservatively and record why.

03

The quantitative stack

Black-Scholes and full greeks verified to 50-digit references, IV solvers, SVI surfaces with arbitrage screens, Heston & SABR, Gaussian/Student-t/Clayton/Gumbel copulas, GARCH & Markov-regime vol, Kalman filtering, option-correct VaR, and historical crisis replay (GFC, COVID, rate shocks).

04

Statistical gatekeeping

The credibility centerpiece: deflated Sharpe ratios, conformal prediction with finite-sample coverage guarantees, walk-forward validation, and an edge-attestation gate. Fed pure noise thousands of times, it blessed none of it. No provable edge, no capital.

05

Multi-tenant control plane & security

Encrypted credential vaulting (Fernet/scrypt, later KMS with rotation), tenant isolation, authenticated APIs, hard per-tenant loss envelopes, and a sandboxed BYO-strategy pipeline that survived reflection attacks, import bombs, and multi-stage code execution in audit.

06

Live-trading safety

Engineered on the assumption everything fails: halt-and-flatten that never leaves a leg open, idempotent orders across crash-restarts, broker adapters that fail loud on API drift, kill switches reachable from every loop state, and reconciliation that trusts the broker over local state.

07

Productionization

The operating discipline: a go-live gate, deployment & disaster-recovery tooling, observability and alerting, EOD pipelines, paper-trading soak tests, dress-rehearsal onboarding, billing & treasury, trade surveillance, and a SEBI compliance pack.

The differentiator

Five adversarial audits. Up to fifteen parallel lanes each.

What distinguishes this engagement isn't what was built but how it was verified. Independent reviewers re-derived the platform's math from first principles: high-precision autodiff against every greek, two-million-sample Monte Carlo against every copula tail, zero-slack coverage simulation against every conformal guarantee.

The audits found real problems, and that's the point. Each was fixed with a regression test that fails on the old code, then re-verified by a reviewer whose only job was to refute the fix. Caught and closed before they could touch capital:

A volatility model that silently accepted a failed optimizer fit
A live-halt path that could leave restored positions open at the broker
A sandbox escape via reflection
A config combination that could onboard a live tenant with no loss caps
A what-if tool that reported zero risk for a position it had silently dropped

The final audit cycles returned zero critical and zero high-severity findings on the quantitative core.

The takeaway

Anyone can build a backtest that looks good. The engineering problem worth paying for is a system that is incapable of lying to you, about fills, about risk, about whether an edge is real.
