Here is a question that kept coming up in a conversation we had with a fund manager earlier this year:

"The Indian F&O market has 206 eligible stocks. I can realistically watch maybe 10 at a time. The other 196 are opportunities I am completely blind to."

That is not a discipline problem. That is a physics problem. Human attention is finite. The market is not.

So we built a system that solves it.

The EOS Strategy — Exhaustion of Strength

At the core of the system is a contrarian thesis: when a stock makes a sharp move — up or down — with heavy institutional participation behind it, that move is often reaching its limit. The smart money got in early. Late buyers are piling in now. The reversal conditions are ripe.

When a stock surges more than 2% with significant Open Interest buildup, the system buys a Put. When it drops under the same conditions, it buys a Call. It never enters blindly — it waits for confirmation on the next candle.

It applies this logic to all 206 F&O stocks. Simultaneously. In milliseconds.

The part that makes it different from every other algo

Before any trade is entered, the system consults an AI model. Not to predict the market — but to review the signal in context. Is this a good time to be buying options? Does the broader market support this direction? Does anything look off?

The AI approves or rejects the trade with a confidence score. Its reasoning is logged in full. Every decision is auditable.

This acts as a second set of eyes on every decision — the kind of quality filter a pure rule-based system simply cannot provide.

What it adds up to: A system that applies the same logic, the same discipline, and the same risk rules to every trade — across all 206 stocks — with no fatigue, no emotion, no inconsistency. The kind of execution human traders cannot physically sustain.

Where it is headed next

The next phase is a Market Analyser that wakes at 5:45 AM on every trading day, ingests global and domestic market data, classifies the market regime, and delivers a written AI briefing with a prioritised watchlist by 8:00 AM — before the market opens.

The goal: a system that requires zero human intervention from pre-market preparation to end-of-day reconciliation on a typical trading day.
