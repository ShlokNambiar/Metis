Short answer

Renewal and servicing calls belong on the 1600 series. New business and cross-sell belong on 140. Ordinary 10-digit mobile numbers are restricted for commercial calling, and the deadline to move was 1 January 2026.

If your renewals desk still dials policyholders from ordinary mobile numbers, you are outside the rules — and have been since January. Most brokers we speak to do not know this changed. Most vendors selling them dialers have not mentioned it.

There is also a part nobody mentions, and it is the part that should actually make you move: compliance will probably raise your connect rate, not cost you one.

What changed

TRAI notified amendments to the Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations (TCCCPR) on 12 February 2025, published in the Gazette on 18 February 2025. Most provisions took effect 30 days after publication; Regulations 8, 17, 20(a–b) and 21(b) took 60 days.

The headline change is simple, and it is the one that catches brokers:

Ordinary 10-digit numbers are restricted for commercial calling.

Not "restricted above a certain volume." Not "restricted for bulk campaigns." Commercial calling moves to designated number series, and the compliance deadline for adoption was 1 January 2026. That date has passed.

The distinction that matters for brokers

Renewal reminders to an existing policyholder are service communications. They belong on 1600, and policy renewals are named explicitly as a use case for the series.

Calls to someone who is not yet your customer are promotional. Those belong on 140, and they require DND scrubbing before every single call plus a verifiable consent record.

If you are running both kinds of call off the same regular mobile numbers — which is what most renewals desks do — you have a compliance problem on both halves at once.

The upside nobody mentions

Here is the part that turns this from a cost into an advantage.

Calls originating from the 1600 series cannot be tagged, blocked, or filtered. Customers cannot DND them away. Handset spam-labelling does not apply.

Think about what renewal calling looks like today from a regular 10-digit number: an unknown mobile, often flagged "Spam Risk" by the recipient's phone, competing with every other unknown caller for attention.

Now consider the same call from a number the network is required to deliver, that cannot be silently filtered.

Moving renewal calling to 1600 is not a compliance tax. It is a deliverability upgrade that happens to also be the law.

What does non-compliance cost?

The amendments tightened both the complaint mechanism and the penalties.

Complaints got easier to file and faster to bite

  • The complaint window was extended to 7 days
  • The action threshold dropped to 5 complaints within 10 days — previously 10 complaints within 7 days

Financial disincentives on access providers

These sit on your telecom provider, and flow down to you through them.

  • ₹1,000 per valid complaint wrongly dismissed
  • ₹5,000 per improper header or content template registration
  • Misreporting: ₹2 lakh first instance, ₹5 lakh second, ₹10 lakh subsequent

Service suspension for repeated violations

  • First offence: 15-day bar on outgoing services
  • Subsequent: one-year disconnection and blacklisting across all providers

Read that last line again. Not a fine — a year, across every operator.

And there is a second regulator

Non-compliance may draw action not only from TRAI but from sector regulators including IRDAI, RBI and SEBI.

For a broker, that is the real exposure. A telecom penalty is a cost. An IRDAI file is a different category of problem, and it lands on the licence you spent years obtaining.

What to actually do

  1. Separate your call types. Renewals, payment reminders and policy servicing are service calls. New business, cross-sell and lead follow-up are promotional. Most desks currently run both through one queue on one set of numbers. That has to split.
  2. Get onto the right series. Service calling to 1600 through your provider. Promotional calling to 140.
  3. Register on DLT. Every commercial caller must be registered on the Distributed Ledger Technology platform before making outbound calls. Headers and content templates register there too.
  4. Scrub DND before every promotional call. Not once a month. Before every call. This does not apply to your 1600 traffic.
  5. Keep verifiable consent records. Explicit consent must be captured in a robust and verifiable manner. Note the sharp limit: explicit consent for service communications facilitating a transaction is valid for a maximum of 7 days. Consent gathered last year does not cover a call you make today.
  6. Respect calling hours. Promotional calls are restricted to permitted hours.

Where AI voice agents fit

If you are evaluating AI calling for renewals, the regulation should be your first question to any vendor, not your last.

An AI agent making thousands of calls a day from ordinary 10-digit numbers is not a clever workaround. It is the same violation at higher volume, with a clearer audit trail — and the complaint threshold is now five in ten days.

Questions worth asking any vendor:

  • Which number series will my calls originate from?
  • Are you registered on DLT, or am I expected to be?
  • How is DND scrubbing handled on promotional traffic, and how often?
  • How are consent records captured and stored, and for how long?
  • If a complaint is filed, what evidence can you produce?

If a vendor cannot answer the first question immediately, that tells you what you need to know. We wrote the longer version of this as ten questions to ask before buying an AI calling agent in India.

Common questions

Is it illegal to call customers from a normal mobile number in India?

For commercial communications, ordinary 10-digit numbers are restricted. Commercial calling belongs on the designated series — 140 for promotional, and 1600 for service and transactional calls by regulated BFSI entities. The adoption deadline was 1 January 2026.

Do insurance renewal reminders count as promotional calls?

No. Renewal reminders to an existing policyholder are service communications and belong on the 1600 series. Cross-selling a different product to that same customer is promotional and belongs on the 140 series.

Can customers block calls from the 1600 series?

No. Calls originating from the 1600 series cannot be tagged, blocked or filtered, and DND preferences do not apply to them. Customers can block 140-series promotional calls through the DND registry.

Does TRAI's number series rule apply if we only make a few hundred calls a day?

Yes. The restriction on using ordinary 10-digit numbers for commercial calling is not volume-based. It applies regardless of how many calls you place.

What are the penalties for non-compliant commercial calling in India?

Action is triggered by five valid complaints within ten days. A first offence carries a 15-day bar on outgoing services; repeat violations carry one-year disconnection and blacklisting across all providers. Sector regulators including IRDAI, RBI and SEBI may also act independently of TRAI.

Does using an AI voice agent change TCCCPR obligations?

No. The obligations attach to the commercial communication itself, not to whether a human or an automated system places the call. An AI agent calling from non-compliant numbering is the same violation at greater volume.
